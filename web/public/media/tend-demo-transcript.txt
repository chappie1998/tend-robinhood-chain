Tend — captured product demonstration
September 12, 2026

A view on the market. A limit on your loss.
A trader can be right about the final price and still be liquidated along the way. Tend gives BTC
and ETH traders a different contract: a fixed premium, capped upside, and no liquidation. This is
the actual app running on Monad testnet.

Two markets. Real exchange data.
Switch from Bitcoin to Ethereum and the market chart updates with real Coinbase prices. The data
path checks freshness and rejects missing or malformed prices. A data failure stops quoting instead
of inventing a number.

Choose direction. Review the exact expiry.
Choose up or down, an expiry schedule, and the premium you are willing to lose. The exact expiry is
shown because a scheduled market is not a new full duration from purchase. Twenty-five times means
maximum payout divided by premium, not a guaranteed return.

The maximum payout is escrowed at entry.
At the fill, the vault locks the position's maximum payout and enforces its risk limits. The pool
view reads assets, locked collateral, and open obligations directly from Monad. Here, the completed
rehearsal has settled and its collateral is released.

Inspect one completed trade.
This is historical position ten, completed earlier today through the live quote API and a command-
line wallet. The buyer paid just under one hundred mock USDC. The vault escrowed two thousand five
hundred mock USDC, and the receipt records the checks.

A real fill, with a public transaction hash.
MonadScan shows the successful fill and the exact premium transfer into the vault. The rehearsal
reconciled the buyer's balance and collateral lock. This is an actual testnet transaction, not a
simulated success screen.

The loss case is part of the proof.
After the real expiry, the position settled successfully with a payout of zero. The premium was the
entire trading loss, excluding network gas, and collateral returned to the pool. The demo uses a
real Coinbase expiry-minute price through MockPyth, which does not authenticate oracle attestations.

Capped exposure. Defined downside. Built on Monad.
September work adds model-based pricing, expiry ladders, indicative marks, and realized trade
history to an existing foundation. Monad supports the EVM contracts and short-lived signed quote
flow shown here. This remains a testnet demo: mock collateral, mock oracle verification, no early
exit, and a keeper scheduling gap. Explore the app and inspect the receipts at monad dot usetend dot
xyz.
